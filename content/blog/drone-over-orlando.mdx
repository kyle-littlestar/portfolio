I didn't plan this trip as a shoot. I planned it as a test — of my gear, of my knowledge of the airspace, and honestly of my own patience for bureaucracy. What I got back was footage I'm still thinking about.

## Stop One — Lake Eola Park

Downtown Orlando sits inside Class D airspace. That means before a single prop spins, you need authorization — I used LAANC through the FAA's DroneZone to get approval for the altitude I needed, and made sure my drone was registered with my FAA registration number marked on the body. Not optional, not a technicality. If you're flying anywhere near a controlled airport, this is the floor, not the ceiling.

Lake Eola is a stunning subject from above — the fountain at center, the amphitheater, the walking path ringing the lake like a clock face. But downtown also means people, and people mean you're visible. I kept altitude conservative, avoided flying directly over crowds, and stayed well within my approved ceiling.

<Video src="/blog/drone-orlando/lake-eola.mp4" />

About twenty minutes into the session, two Orlando PD officers approached. I'll be honest — I welcomed it. I had my FAA registration on me, my LAANC authorization pulled up on my phone, and I knew exactly what I was doing and why it was legal. The conversation lasted maybe five minutes. They were professional, curious, and once they saw I had my paperwork and wasn't doing anything reckless, they moved on without issue.

Being registered and prepared isn't just about avoiding a fine. It's the difference between that conversation ending in five minutes versus your equipment getting impounded.

## Stop Two — Park of the Americas

A short drive from Eola, Park of the Americas offered something completely different — less geometry, more texture. The tree canopy here is dense and the sightlines are tighter, which meant flying lower and slower and being much more deliberate about every movement.

<Video src="/blog/drone-orlando/park-of-the-americas.mp4" />

This is where flying patiently pays off. The shots that work here aren't the wide sweeping moves — they're the slow reveals through the canopy, the light catching the tops of the trees, the quiet stuff. Nature doesn't cooperate with aggressive flying.

## Stop Three — Lake Baldwin

Lake Baldwin was the last stop and the one I'll remember longest. It sits outside the controlled airspace pressure of downtown, which opened up both the altitude and the mindset. There's something that shifts when you're no longer running through a mental checklist of restrictions — you start actually *seeing*.

<Video src="/blog/drone-orlando/lake-baldwin.mp4" />

The light at late afternoon here was everything. The lake surface, the tree line, the way the shadows stretched across the water — I was working until I had to pack up because I couldn't see the screen anymore.

---

## What I'd Tell Anyone Flying Downtown for the First Time

Get your authorization before you go, not when you arrive. Know your ceiling. Have your registration number physically on the drone. Carry your paperwork.

None of that is a burden if you treat it like part of the craft. The officers I spoke to weren't looking to ruin anyone's day — they were doing their job. So was I.
